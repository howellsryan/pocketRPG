# World & Environment Design Review — 2026-07

> Review of [skridlevsky/migla](https://github.com/skridlevsky/migla), our own world pipeline and editor, and a plan to get from "AI-generated prop soup" to a realistic, readable 3D world. Review only — no code shipped with this doc. Companions: `docs/open-world-terrain-plan.md`, `docs/world-editor-guide.md`, `docs/open-world-asset-coverage.md`. The enforceable quality bar extracted from this review lives in `.claude/rules/world-design.md` (auto-loads on world-authoring paths).

## 0) Verdict

Migla's world looks better than ours **with a simpler engine**. It has no heightmaps, no blended materials, no instanced scatter — all of which we have. Its quality comes from four things we lack:

1. **A painted ground layer.** Terrain "kinds" (grass, water, dirt path, plank/stone/tile floor) painted as flat-colour regions. Paths lead the eye, water breaks up green, floors read as interiors. Our ground is one material per zone with nothing to follow.
2. **Buildings as data, not prop piles.** Author a floor region + a ring of wall props; the client synthesizes the roof (auto-fitted GLB), doorway gaps become animated doors, roofs fade when you walk inside. We hand-place 200–500 individual props per zone from scripts.
3. **One constrained palette + toon shading.** Warm, saturated, "no pure black/gray", every colour from one file. Our zones pick palette/ambience ad hoc.
4. **Curation with a screenshot gate.** One small hand-curated world; an agent "foreman" refuses merges without live browser screenshots. We have `shoot-zone.mjs` but no mandatory pass and no pass/fail criteria.

None of this needs new tech we don't have. It needs one zone-format feature (ground paint), one renderer feature (building synthesis), editor tools on top, and a written quality bar that AI sessions must meet.

## 1) Migla — what it is and what to take

**Facts** (from the repo): browser MMO, Rust deterministic sim + authoritative WS server, Three.js client, ~63% Rust / 37% TS, almost entirely written by Claude Code agents under one human. World lives in one hand-edited `worlds/hearthmead.world.json` (256×256 tiles), compiled by a `mapbake` tool to binary; CI fails if source and bake drift.

**World format** — simpler than ours: a `points` array (props with rotation + blocking footprints, resource nodes with respawn ticks, enemy dens, one spawn) plus rectangular `zones` with safe/wild flags, plus rectangular **terrain regions** typed as one of 12 kinds (`client/src/terrain.ts`): grass, water, plank/stone/tile floor, path kinds, etc. Last region wins; each kind is a flat colour from a single warm palette; interior floor kinds don't affect collision.

**Buildings** (`client/src/buildings.ts`) are client-synthesized render sugar: detect floor-kind rectangles, find the wall-prop ring one tile out, fit one of three roof GLBs (thatch gable / tile gable / tile hip) spanning interior + walls + 0.35-tile eaves, ridge along the long axis, height clamped 0.8–2.0×. Gaps in the wall ring become swinging doors (1.75 rad at 7 rad/s). Roofs fade over ~200 ms when the player is inside. No collision or server involvement.

**Take**: ground paint, building synthesis, palette discipline, screenshot-gated authoring, "small and curated beats big and generated".
**Skip**: Rust rewrite, binary map bake (our D1-stored JSON + `validateZone` already covers drift), the single-shared-world model (our 14-place geography is a feature), and their 80 MB PNG icon folder (an anti-lesson they flag themselves).

## 2) Why our AI-generated zones look bad — diagnosis

Verified against `world/zones/*.json`, `world/scripts/gen-*.mjs`, `world/shared/zone.ts`, `world/client/src/`:

1. **No ground variation.** `ZoneDef` has one 4-colour checker palette + one terrain material preset blended by elevation/slope. There is no way to author a road, a plaza, a riverbed, or an interior floor. Every zone is a green field with props on it; nothing guides the eye anywhere.
2. **No water.** Terrain plan T5's water plane was never built, yet 6 of the 14 planned places are coastal/marsh/river (Port Sarin, Barlock, Catherra, Draynar, Canifel, Faloden's river crossing).
3. **Buildings are prop assemblages with no composition rules.** Varrick is 519 script-placed props. The gen scripts place landmarks at coordinates but have no concept of streets, lots, building orientation, or foreground/background rhythm — so output reads as scattered objects, not a town.
4. **Palette and ambience are per-zone guesses.** Nothing ties zone palettes to `DESIGN.md`/`biomes3d.json`; adjacent zones can disagree on what "grass" looks like.
5. **The process bypasses both the editor and the eye.** Recent zones (Varrick, Lumbright, the dungeon) were generated by `gen-*.mjs` scripts and committed after a single optional screenshot look. The editor — built precisely for this — was not in the loop, and there is no written definition of "good enough to ship".

Point 5 answers "are we just not using our designer?": **yes**. The editor works and is deployed, but zone authoring moved to scripts because scripts are reproducible and agent-friendly. The fix is not to abandon scripts — it's to make scripts produce **block-outs** that get finished and reviewed in the editor, and to give the editor the two tools that matter most (ground paint, terrain brush).

## 3) The world editor — review and improvement plan

**What's already good** (`docs/world-editor-guide.md`, `world/client/src/editor/`, ~2.7k lines): data-derived asset catalog (new game content appears automatically), collision brushes with rectangle/flood fill, scatter brush with jitter, stamps/prefabs, portal tool with cross-zone arrival picker, undo/redo + localStorage drafts, server-side validation, revision history, save-to-preview with instant play-test teleport, publish-to-production, and a read-only 3D preview using the real renderer. This is a genuinely strong foundation — better than migla's `worldedit.ts`.

**Improvements, in priority order** (P0 items are what unblock quality; UI/UX items follow):

| # | Improvement | Why / notes |
|---|---|---|
| P0-1 | **Ground-paint tool** — brush/rect/fill painting terrain kinds (path, plaza, water, farm dirt, interior floors) onto a new per-tile ground layer (§4.1) | The single biggest visual win, migla-proven. Editor UI is the same brush interaction the collision tools already have. |
| P0-2 | **Heightmap brush** (raise/lower/smooth/flatten) + per-zone PNG export | Already planned as terrain-plan T5; today relief is procedural-noise-only, so nobody can author a river valley or a castle mound. |
| P0-3 | **In-editor review mode** — one button that captures the 3D preview from 3–4 canonical angles (spawn view, landmark view, each exit) and shows them side by side | Makes the §5 screenshot gate a 10-second action instead of a script run. |
| P1-4 | **Building tool** — draw a floor rect, pick wall/roof style, editor emits floor paint + wall ring + door gap; renderer synthesizes roof/door (§4.2) | Towns in minutes; replaces hand-assembling buildings from props. |
| P1-5 | **Path tool** — click a polyline, editor paints a path of chosen width with soft edges | Roads are the skeleton of every good zone; painting them tile-by-tile is too slow. |
| P1-6 | Library **thumbnails** (render each catalog GLB to a sprite once at build) instead of text rows | Finding "the right tree" among 40 props is currently blind. |
| P1-7 | **Walk-through preview** — WASD/click-move camera in the 3D preview | Ground-level composition (sightlines, scale) is invisible from top-down. |
| P2-8 | Reference-image side panel (pin concept art/screenshots while editing) | Cheap, keeps authoring anchored to a target. |
| P2-9 | Zone-revision visual diff (render rev A vs rev B side by side) | Review aid once multiple people/agents author. |
| P2-10 | Script↔editor round-trip: `gen-*.mjs` emits a draft revision to the editor instead of the final JSON | Formalizes "scripts block out, humans/editor finish". |

**Off-the-shelf instead?** Re-checked the world-editor-plan survey: Tiled/LDtk/Sprite Fusion still can't express our typed entities, portal graphs, D1 publish flow, or 3D preview. Extending our editor remains right. For *terrain sculpting only*, authoring heightmap PNGs in any image editor already works today (T4 path) — the brush just internalizes it.

## 4) New format/renderer features (the enablers)

These are the code features the plan needs — listed for scoping, not built here:

### 4.1 Painted ground layer
Add an optional `ground` block to `ZoneDef`: a list of typed regions (rects and/or brush-painted runs), kinds drawn from a small registry (`path_dirt`, `path_cobble`, `plaza`, `water`, `farm`, `floor_plank`, `floor_stone`, …), last-region-wins like migla. Renderer: vertex-colour (or low-res splat texture) the **existing** terrain mesh — no new geometry, works with the current blended material as an overriding layer. Water kind additionally gets a translucent animated plane at its level and darkens the heightfield beneath. Collision stays exactly where it is (water tiles are authored blocked in the ASCII grid). Absent block = today's rendering, so all zones keep working.

### 4.2 Building synthesis
Migla's recipe, adapted: floor-kind region + wall props ringing it → auto-fitted roof GLB (we already own gable/hip roofs in the KayKit Medieval Builder + Kenney Fantasy Town kits), doorway gaps → door meshes, interior roof fade. Client-render-only, same contract as props.

### 4.3 Art direction constants
One module of named zone palettes + ambience presets per biome (mirroring `biomes3d.json` and `DESIGN.md` tokens), replacing per-zone hex guesses. Warm and saturated; no pure greys/blacks (migla's rule, and it shows). A **toon-ramp/stylized lighting pass** is worth a spike after ground paint lands — flat-colour assets (KayKit/Kenney/Quaternius) read dramatically better toon-shaded than under plain PBR lighting.

### 4.4 Ambient life
Non-combat critters (Bunny/Birb/Pigeon/sheep — already matched in `docs/open-world-asset-coverage.md`), chimney smoke, birds. Cheap wander NPCs with no combat; towns feel inhabited.

## 5) Process: the screenshot gate and the block-out rule

Codified in `.claude/rules/world-design.md` (auto-loads whenever an agent touches zones, gen scripts, or the editor). Summary:

- **Gen scripts produce block-outs, not shipped zones.** Layout, collision, landmarks, exits from the script; dressing, ground paint, and final composition finished/reviewed via the editor or an explicit screenshot pass.
- **No zone ships without a screenshot review** (`node scripts/shoot-zone.mjs <zone>` minimum; editor review mode once built) against a concrete checklist: visible landmark, paths that lead somewhere, clustered-not-uniform density, dressed edges, approved palette, no floating/intersecting props, exits framed and labelled.
- **Composition before decoration**: landmark first, then roads, then buildings oriented to roads, then props in clusters with deliberate clearings, then scatter.

## 7) Delivery plan

Phases are independently shippable; each gates on `world:check` + the §5 screenshot review. No dates — sized in agent-sessions.

- **W0 — Art direction + rules (done in this review):** quality-bar rule shipped; palette/ambience preset consolidation is the first code task.
- **W1 — Ground paint + water (format → renderer → editor tool):** §4.1, then repaint the existing 5 zones with roads/plazas/water. Expected to be the visible step-change.
- **W2 — Building synthesis + town re-dress:** §4.2; rebuild Varrick and Lumbright's buildings with it; add ambient life (§4.4).
- **W3 — Editor batch:** heightmap brush, review mode, path tool, thumbnails, walk-through preview (P0-2→P1-7).
- **W4 — Per-place authoring pass:** the terrain-plan §6 table of 14 places, each authored to the checklist with screenshot sign-off; buy KayKit EXTRA/Quaternius Patreon as gaps surface.
- **Spike (any time after W1):** toon-ramp lighting A/B, screenshots to `DESIGN.md` review.
