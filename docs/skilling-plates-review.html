The Skilling Plates

Three reference plates — a hero mining an ore seam, felling a tree, and drawing a fish from a river. Built from scratch, not adapted from the existing skilling stage. These set the figure, the tool standard and the motion vocabulary every other skill inherits, so they are drawn to the bar the weapon profiles set: real joints, forged tools with beveled edges and bound grips, and environments with strata, bark and current.

Every strike point here was measured, not eyeballed: each tool carries an invisible marker at its working tip, the animation is frozen at the impact frame, and the marker's real stage position is read back out of the browser. The ore seam, the notch and every chip and spark are then placed on that measured point. The first draft guessed, and the pick landed 30 px under the floor.

PLATE 01

Mining the seam

A pick is swung from over the shoulder and stopped dead by the rock. The whole read is in the stop: the body keeps travelling for two frames after the head lands.

Dope sheet1000 ms
1Anticipation0–320
2Downswing320–430
3Impact430–540
4Recovery540–1000

What to look at

  • The stop. Shoulder and elbow reverse hard at 430 ms; the torso overshoots to 470 and settles. That two-frame lag is the whole weight read.
  • Seam glow pulses on contact — the rock is the thing giving way, so it reacts, not just the particles.
  • Chips carry ore colour. Two of the six are verdigris, so what shatters matches what you're mining.
  • Rock jolt is 2.4 px and damped over three beats. Any more reads as an earthquake.
PLATE 02

Felling the trunk

Same rig, different physics. An axe travels further round the body than a pick and bites at an angle into a notch that is already there — so the swing is wider, the recoil softer, and the tree answers all the way up in the canopy.

Dope sheet1150 ms
1Wind-up0–415
2Swing415–520
3Bite520–645
4Recovery645–1150

What to look at

  • A wider arc than the pick — the shoulder travels 122° against mining's 104°, because an axe is swung round the body, not dropped on the spot.
  • The tree answers as one object. Trunk, boughs and canopy pivot together at the root, so the shiver travels up rather than the leaves twitching alone.
  • Heartwood brightens on the bite and the notch stays cut between swings — the damage persists, so the loop reads as progress.
  • Ambient sway runs at 5.2 s, deliberately out of phase with the 1.15 s swing so the tree never looks metronomic.
PLATE 03

Drawing the river

The one skill with no strike. Fishing is a wait that pays: a long hold on a bobbing float, a bite, a set, and a fish hauled clear of the surface. This is the template for every skill whose action is patience rather than force.

Dope sheet5200 ms
1Hold0–2600
2Bite2600–3120
3Set & haul3120–3900
4Landing3900–4520
5Reset4520–5200

What to look at

  • The blank bends as a chain — three segments at 9°, 14° and 20° compound into a real curve. A single rotated rod reads as a stick tilting.
  • Half the cycle is the wait. The float bobs, the rod tip sways, nothing else happens. That patience is the point of the skill.
  • The surface film is a layer, not a mask. The trout is drawn under a translucent sheet, so it darkens below the line and clears it on the haul with no clipping.
  • The reel turns only while hauling — 320° over 780 ms, then holds. It is the one part that must not idle.
PROPS

The three tools, isolated

Each authored in its own local frame — origin at the hand, +X toward the working end — the same convention the weapon profiles use, so a tool can be placed by one translate and one rotate and never drifts out of the fist. Drawn here at four times stage scale.

Miner's pickaxe

Hickory haft under a cross-bound leather grip, a steel collar where the head is hung, and a forged crescent that crosses the haft and comes to a point at both ends. The head is taller than it is wide, which is what stops a pick silhouetting as a spear — the first attempt did.

grip (0,0) · eye x=87–107 · points (84,−31) and (99,32) · 63 tall on a 119 haft

Felling axe

A haft with real curve and a fawn's-foot knob so the hand has something to pull against. The head is an eye block plus a bit with a STRAIGHT top edge running on from the haft, the mass dropping into a beard below it — asymmetry plus a small head on a long haft (about 4:1, as a real felling axe is) is what makes it an axe rather than a spade. Polish is carried only on the middle of the edge, where a working axe is actually sharpened. This is the weakest of the three and the one I'd most like a steer on — it took six passes; the silhouette is right now, but of the three this is the one I would still hand to a proper illustrator.

grip (0,0) · eye x=84–98 · flat top (95,−10)→(109.5,−11.5) · beard to (98.5,19.5) · 30 tall on a 110 haft

River rod

Speckled cork over a brass reel seat, a spoked centre-pin, and a blank in three segments so it can bend. Wire guides are whipped in red thread at the seams — the detail that says "rod" faster than the taper does.

butt (0,0) · seat x=20–34 · joints x=96 / 150 / 186 · tip ring (228, −2.6)

What these commit us to

Approving these three fixes four things for every skill that follows: one articulated figure with fourteen joints, tools authored in their own local frame, environments built from real materials rather than a tinted silhouette, and a four-beat motion grammar — anticipation, action, impact, recovery — that a smith's hammer, a fletcher's knife and a runecrafter's channel all fit inside. Nothing is wired into the game yet.
